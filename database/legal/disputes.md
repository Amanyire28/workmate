## 1. What WorkMate can and can't do

1.1 WorkMate **can**:

- keep the messages, quotations and job history;
- review evidence and ask both sides questions;
- warn, restrict, suspend or remove accounts;
- hide reviews or content that break our rules;
- give records to the police or a court when lawfully asked.

1.2 WorkMate **can't**:

- refund money it never received;
- force anyone to pay;
- decide legal liability between users;
- guarantee any outcome.

## 2. When the customer cancels

2.1 Before work starts: cancel through WorkMate. Any deposit is handled as agreed. If nothing was agreed, the provider should return it, less reasonable costs they already incurred, such as materials bought for the job, with receipts. After work starts: the customer pays for work done and materials used, as agreed. [[advocate: ETA s.25 seven-day cancellation and the s.24(7)(d) exception]]

## 3. When the provider cancels

3.1 Tell the customer at once through WorkMate and return any payment for work not done. Late or repeated cancellations count against the provider's record and can lead to restrictions.

## 4. No-shows

4.1 Report a no-show from the job. First no-show: a warning. Repeated no-shows within [[90 days]]: the provider is hidden from search or suspended. Customers who repeatedly aren't there when the provider arrives may be restricted too.

## 5. Unfinished work

5.1 The provider should finish or agree a fair settlement for the part done. Report it with photos if they don't.

## 6. Poor-quality work

6.1 Give the provider a chance to fix it first (SGSSA s.18). Report it with photos and messages if it isn't fixed. We may warn or restrict the provider. Claims for money are between the two of you, or for a court.

## 7. Damage to property

7.1 Photograph the damage straight away and report it. The provider is responsible for damage caused by their negligence (Provider Agreement clause 16). WorkMate will ask for the provider's response and may suspend them during the review if there is evidence of serious damage.

## 8. Disagreements about payment

8.1 Show the agreed price from messages or the quotation, and proof of payment. WorkMate compares what was agreed in the app. Agreements made outside the app are much harder for us to review.

## 9. Suspected fraud

9.1 Report it at once and also report to the police. We may suspend the account immediately to protect others, keep the records for the investigation (DPPA s.18(2)(a)), and share them with the police when lawfully asked.

## 10. Asking for a refund

10.1 Ask the provider in WorkMate messages, saying how much and why. If they refuse, report it as a payment problem. WorkMate may contact the provider and record the outcome, but the refund comes from the provider.

## 11. Sending evidence

11.1 Attach up to 3 photos to a report, and answer the safety team's questions in the report. Don't edit or fake evidence, because that is a breach of the Terms.

## 12. How we review a complaint

12.1 We confirm we received it, review it, may ask either side a question (shown as "Your answer needed"), and decide. We aim to reply within [[7]] days, and faster for safety issues. We record complaints and their outcomes and report a summary to the Personal Data Protection Office where required (DPP Regs 40, 50).

## 13. Talking to each other

13.1 Keep it in WorkMate messages, polite and factual. We may close the chat if either side becomes abusive.

## 14. Escalation

14.1 If you disagree with our decision, ask for a review within [[14]] days by replying in the report. A senior staff member reviews it. Beyond WorkMate, you can go to:

- the police, for crimes;
- local council or community mediation, or a court;
- the Personal Data Protection Office, for privacy issues.

## 15. Possible outcomes

15.1 No action, advice to both sides, a warning, review removal, restriction, suspension or permanent removal. We tell the reporter the outcome. The reported person is told about any action taken against their account, but not who reported them.
